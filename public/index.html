How the model works

  • Projects: each stage is recognised as revenue in its invoice month, and its sub-contractor cost is charged to cost of sales in the same month. The client pays after the stage's payment days (30 days counts as one month, rounded to whole months). Sub-contractors are paid in the month the client pays.
  • Recurring revenue streams start at the first-month figure and grow by a fixed percentage each month. Customers pay after the receivable days.
  • Salaries and operating costs start from the figures you enter and can be changed month by month. Salaries are paid in the month they are incurred and carry no VAT. Other operating costs carry VAT and are paid after the payable days.
  • VAT applies to all sales and costs except salaries. Receivables and payables include VAT. VAT is paid in March, June, September and December. Each payment covers the quarter that ended the month before, so VAT from the payment month itself (December, for example) goes into the next quarter's payment.
  • The cash flow forecast shows money in and out in the month it happens, laid out like the P&L: customer receipts, payments for cost of sales and operating costs, VAT, interest and tax, then investing and financing. Amounts are excluding VAT, with VAT shown on its own lines. Recurring revenue and costs follow the receivable and payable days, so each recurring line is its share of the month's total cash.
  • Depreciation is straight-line over the asset life, starting the month after purchase, plus the existing depreciation figure.
  • Corporate tax is charged on positive monthly profit before tax, with no carry-forward of losses, and paid after the tax lag.
  • Years run January to December, so the first year covers three months (Oct to Dec 2026).
